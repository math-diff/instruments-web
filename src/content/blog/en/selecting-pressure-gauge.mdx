Pressure gauges are the most common field instrument, yet the classic
failure modes — bent pointers, ruptured Bourdon tubes, drifting
readings — keep showing up in after-sales cases. This article walks
through the five selection decisions that matter most in engineering
practice for mechanical pressure gauges.

## 1. Range: leave a one-third margin

The fundamental rule of pressure gauge range selection: **normal
operating pressure should sit between one-third and two-thirds of
span**.

- Steady pressure: normal pressure ideally at 50–67% of span;
- Pulsating service (pump discharge): normal pressure should not
  exceed two-thirds of span, leaving margin for surges;
- Continuous operation near the top of span accelerates fatigue of
  the elastic element (elastic hysteresis); operation below one-third
  of span reduces effective resolution and increases relative error.

Example: pump discharge at 0.9 MPa normally, surging to 1.1 MPa —
a 0–1.6 MPa gauge (normal at ~56% of span) is correct. A 0–1.0 MPa
gauge would be over-ranged; a 0–2.5 MPa gauge would read in the lower
third of the dial.

## 2. Accuracy class: fit for purpose

Industrial pressure gauges per GB/T 1226:2017 (general-purpose pressure
gauges) come in classes 1.0, 1.6, 2.5 and 4.0. The class number is
the **percentage of full-span error** — a class 1.6 gauge on a
0–1.6 MPa range has a permissible error of ±1.6% × 1.6 MPa =
±0.026 MPa.

| Service | Recommended class |
| --- | --- |
| General monitoring (compressed air, cooling water) | 2.5 |
| General process lines, pump discharge | 1.6 |
| Custody transfer, test benches | 1.0 or better |

Note that higher classes cost more and are more sensitive to mounting
attitude and vibration. Specifying an unnecessarily high class does
not improve system reliability.

## 3. Sensing element by media and range

- **Bourdon tube (C-spring)**: the general-purpose element for gases,
  liquids and steam from roughly 0.1 MPa up to 60 MPa. Ranges like
  0–1.6 MPa and 0–2.5 MPa cover most line monitoring. 316L stainless
  wetted parts handle water, steam and mildly corrosive media.
- **Capsule (corrugated diaphragm)**: for low and very low pressures,
  from a few hundred pascals up to tens of kPa — gas regulator
  monitoring, furnace draft. Typical accuracy class 2.5.
- **Special media**: ammonia service **requires an ammonia-rated
  gauge** (different Bourdon-tube material to avoid ammonia stress
  corrosion); oxygen service **requires an oil-free oxygen gauge**
  marked "oil-free" — a safety requirement, not an option.

## 4. Service protection: pulsation, vibration and heat

After-sales statistics show the top three causes of gauge failure are
pulsation shock, ambient vibration and over-temperature media — not
manufacturing defects:

- **Pressure pulsation**: install a **snubber (restrictor or needle
  orifice)** on pump discharge and compressor lines, or specify a
  **liquid-filled (glycerin-damped) vibration-resistant gauge**.
- **Ambient vibration**: choose the damped case, or isolate with a
  flexible siphon / remote mounting.
- **Hot media** (steam above ~60 °C reaching the Bourdon tube
  directly damages the movement): install a **siphon (pigtail) loop**
  so steam condenses before reaching the element.
- **Corrosion**: specify 316L or Hastelloy wetted parts, or a
  **diaphragm-seal (chemical) gauge** where the diaphragm isolates
  the media and a filled capillary transmits the pressure.

## 5. Connection, case and certification

- **Connection orientation**: lower-back (radial) is most common;
  panel mounting uses axial connection with back flange.
- **Case diameter**: 100 mm and 150 mm are standard; 60 mm for dense
  panel layouts.
- **Ingress protection**: IP54 suffices for general indoor service;
  outdoor dusty/wet locations use IP65 liquid-filled cases.
- **Safety release**: the case back must provide a **pressure-relief
  opening**, so a ruptured Bourdon tube vents through the back
  instead of bursting the front glass. GB/T 1226 specifies case
  construction and relief requirements — check the relief opening
  is unobstructed (do not tape over the back).
- **Legal metrology**: gauges used for safety protection or custody
  transfer fall under mandatory verification — schedule periodic
  calibration accordingly.

## Summary

A correct gauge selection = correct range margin + adequate accuracy
class + element matched to the media + protection against pulsation,
vibration and heat. Put these four items into your inquiry and you
will avoid the vast majority of field problems. When in doubt, send
