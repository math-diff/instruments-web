Level is the most intuitive of process parameters, yet level instruments
come in more varieties than any other — magnetic bypass gauges, radar,
floats and sight glasses each have their own stage. A wrong choice
means unreliable readings at best and seal failures at worst. This
guide follows the decision path used in real projects.

## 1. Ask three questions first

1. **Media**: corrosive? viscous? foaming? crystallizing? Is density
   stable?
2. **Purpose**: local indication only, or transmission to the DCS and
   interlocks?
3. **Vessel**: any agitator or heating coil? Is a bypass chamber
   mounting acceptable? Pressure/temperature rating of the nozzles?

These answers mostly determine the candidates.

## 2. The four families

### Magnetic bypass level gauge

**Principle**: a bypass chamber connects to the vessel flanges
(communicating-vessel principle); a magnetized float inside follows
the level and flips dual-color flags on the external scale (red/white).
Magnetic switches or a 4–20 mA module can be clipped on for alarms
and transmission.

- **Strengths**: bold, unambiguous local display; mechanically
  rugged; nearly maintenance-free. High temperature/pressure and
  corrosive (PTFE-lined) versions cover most services.
- **Limits**: the media **density must be stable** and above the
  float rating — density changes shift the float waterline; viscous,
  crystallizing or fouling media can jam the float.
- **Typical**: storage tanks, drums, boiler-drum local indication,
  dosing tanks.

### Radar (non-contact)

**Principle**: the antenna transmits a microwave toward the surface
and times the echo; level is computed from the flight time, largely
independent of density and (within limits) dielectric constant.

- **Strengths**: **non-contact, no wear**, nearly immune to media
  properties; excellent for corrosive, viscous, hot and hygienic
  services.
- **Limits**: foam (especially conductive foam) absorbs or falsely
  reflects microwaves; the antenna must avoid filling streams,
  agitators, heating coils and wall echoes.
- **Typical**: chemical storage tanks, effluent pits, corrosive
  media requiring continuous transmission.

### Float switches / top-mounted floats

**Principle**: a float rides the surface and actuates magnetic
switches along a guide stem — discrete level points, or a continuous
signal via a shaft coupling.

- **Strengths**: simple, inexpensive; discrete outputs suit
  high/low alarms and interlocks.
- **Limits**: contact type; media should be clean; floats can jam
  with fouling; continuous accuracy is modest.
- **Typical**: high/low alarms on water and oil tanks, simple vessel
  interlocks.

### Sight glass (glass-tube / flat-glass)

**Principle**: the most direct communicating vessel — a glass tube
or flat-glass window shows the liquid column directly.

- **Strengths**: the **most trustworthy** local reading, commonly
  used as the reference against magnetic and radar instruments;
  the designated instrument for boiler drums in many codes.
- **Limits**: glass is fragile; media must not crystallize on or
  attack the glass; high-pressure service requires flat-glass
  versions with protective frames — never bare glass tube.
- **Typical**: boiler water level, cross-check readings on storage
  tanks, sampling confirmation.

## 3. Quick comparison

| | Magnetic bypass | Radar | Float | Sight glass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reading | Local (+ optional TX) | Transmission | Switch / TX | Local only |
| Contact with media | Yes (float in chamber) | **No** | Yes | Yes (media in glass) |
| Corrosive media | Lined versions | Excellent | Moderate | Limited by glass |
| Foam / vapor effect | Small | Large (foam absorbs) | Small | Site-dependent |
| High T/P | Good | Good | Moderate | Flat-glass good |
| Maintenance | Very low | Low | Medium (jam-prone float) | Periodic glass checks |
| Cost | Low–mid | Mid–high | Low | Low |

## 4. Rules of thumb

- **Bold local reading, rugged service** → magnetic bypass gauge;
- **Continuous transmission, dirty/viscous/corrosive media** → radar;
- **Only high/low alarms or interlocks** → float switch;
- **Boiler drums and "seeing is believing" cross-checks** → sight
  glass.

The most common real-world combination is **magnetic gauge for local
+ radar for transmission**: field reading and DCS signal mutually
redundant.
