Selecting a pressure transmitter looks straightforward until you face the spec
sheet. This guide walks through the four decisions that matter most.

## 1. Define your range

Pick a range where your normal operating pressure sits around 50–70% of span.
Running at the very top of the range sacrifices resolution; running at the
bottom sacrifices signal-to-noise. If turndown matters, choose a transmitter
with a wide turndown ratio — the DW-3051 supports up to 100:1.

## 2. Match accuracy to the consequence of error

A ±0.075% transmitter is overkill for a cooling-water header but essential for
custody-transfer or reactor charge measurement. Ask: what does 1% error cost
per year? That sets your accuracy budget.

## 3. Choose wetted materials for the media

- **316L** handles most water, steam and mild chemicals.
- **Hastelloy C** is needed for chlorides, acids and high-chloride brines.
- **Tantalum** for the most aggressive media.

## 4. Pick the output your DCS expects

| Output | Best for |
| --- | --- |
| 4–20 mA | Legacy, simple, robust |
| HART | Smart configuration + diagnostics |
| Modbus RTU | Multi-drop digital networks |
