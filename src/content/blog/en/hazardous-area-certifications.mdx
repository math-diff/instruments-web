Every electrical instrument in an explosive atmosphere carries an Ex
marking on its nameplate — the instrument's license to operate.
Misreading it can fail an inspection at best and plant an incident
at worst. This article explains each segment of the marking under
the GB/T 3836 series (harmonized with IEC 60079) and the selection
logic behind it.

## 1. The fire triangle and the two protection strategies

Combustion or explosion needs three things simultaneously: fuel
(gas/dust), oxygen, and an ignition source (spark or hot surface).
Explosion protection is about **keeping these three apart** — since
electrical instruments cannot avoid carrying energy, the design
strategies are:

1. **Contain the ignition source** — an internal explosion must not
   ignite the surrounding atmosphere (e.g. flameproof Ex d);
2. **Starve the ignition source** — the instrument simply cannot
   store enough energy to ignite (e.g. intrinsic safety Ex i).

## 2. Area classification: gases and dusts use different systems

**Gas/vapour atmospheres (GB/T 3836.14 / IEC 60079-10-1):**

| Zone | Definition | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zone 0 | Explosive gas mixture present **continuously** or for long periods | Inside a closed storage tank |
| Zone 1 | Likely to occur in **normal operation** | Fuel stations, pump rooms |
| Zone 2 | Not likely in normal operation; if it occurs, only briefly | General process areas |

**Combustible dust atmospheres (GB/T 12476 / IEC 60079-10-2):**

| Zone | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Zone 20 | Dust cloud present continuously or frequently |
| Zone 21 | Dust cloud occasionally present in normal operation |
| Zone 22 | Not likely in normal operation, short duration only; dust layers possible |

**The first selection rule: the instrument's protection level must
meet or exceed the zone requirement.** Only Ex ia intrinsic safety is
acceptable in Zone 0; Zone 1 allows Ex d and Ex ib; Zone 2 is the
least restrictive (Ex n / Ex ec acceptable).

## 3. Common protection types compared

| Mark | Name | Principle | Typical equipment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ex d | Flameproof | Enclosure withstands internal explosion and quenches the flame | Junction boxes, switches, power equipment |
| Ex ia / ib | Intrinsic safety | Energy limited so sparks cannot ignite | Transmitters, RTDs/TCs and other low-power instruments |
| Ex e | Increased safety | Enhanced construction prevents sparks and hot spots | Junction boxes, motors |
| Ex n / ec | Non-sparking (Zone 2 only) | No sparks in normal operation | General Zone 2 equipment |
| Ex t | Dust ignition protection by enclosure | Enclosure limits surface temperature and dust ingress | Dust atmospheres |

**The key companion for intrinsic safety (Ex i) is the safety
barrier**: an energy-limiting device installed between the safe area
and the hazardous area. When specifying Ex ia instruments, verify that
the barrier's output parameters (Uo, Io, Po) match the instrument's
input parameters (Ui, Ii, Pi), and that the permitted cable
parameters (Co, Lo) cover the field cable capacitance and inductance.

## 4. Reading a complete Ex nameplate

Example marking of a typical flameproof RTD assembly:

**Ex d IIC T6 Gb**

| Segment | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Ex | Explosion-protected |
| d | Protection type: flameproof |
| IIC | Gas group: Group IIC gases (hydrogen, acetylene — hardest to ignite) |
| T6 | Temperature class: maximum surface temperature ≤ 85 °C |
| Gb | Equipment Protection Level (EPL): suitable for Zone 1 (Gb); only Ga is acceptable in Zone 0 |

**Gas groups**: Group I (mine methane) → IIA (propane-like) → IIB
(ethylene-like) → IIC (hydrogen, acetylene). IIC is the most
demanding and is downward compatible with IIB and IIA areas.

**Temperature classes**: classified by the instrument's maximum
surface temperature, which must stay **below** the ignition
temperature of the site gas:

| Class | Max surface temperature |
| --- | --- |
| T1 | 450 °C |
| T2 | 300 °C |
| T3 | 200 °C |
| T4 | 135 °C |
| T5 | 100 °C |
| T6 | 85 °C |

**Quick rule**: the lower the gas ignition temperature (diethyl ether,
for example, is T6 territory), the higher the required temperature
class; a hydrogen atmosphere = Group IIC plus a high temperature
class — the most stringent combination.

## 5. Certification systems and documentation

- **China**: CCC certification (mandatory; explosion-protected
  electrical equipment included since 2020), per the GB/T 3836
  series, certified by authorized testing bodies.
- **International**: IECEx (multi-country mutual recognition);
  European CE-ATEX (Directive 2014/34/EU); North American FM/UL
  (Division system with a different zoning concept).
- **Three things to check at purchase**: the certificate number and
  validity, nameplate marking matching the purchase order, and the
  **termination and sealing requirements** in the installation
  instructions (flameproof joints must never be painted or damaged).
