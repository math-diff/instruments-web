If your instrument sits in a classified area, the certification markings on
the nameplate are not optional decoration — they are the legal basis for
installation. Here is a concise reference.

## Zones and divisions

| Zone | Description | Typical marking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zone 0 | Continuous explosive atmosphere | Ex ia |
| Zone 1 | Likely in normal operation | Ex d / Ex ib |
| Zone 2 | Unlikely, short duration | Ex n / Ex ec |

## Ex d vs Ex i — the key difference

- **Ex d (flameproof)** contains the explosion inside a heavy enclosure. The
  housing survives an internal ignition and cools escaping gases below the
  ignition temperature.
- **Ex i (intrinsic safety)** limits electrical energy so the circuit **cannot**
  produce a spark hot enough to ignite.

## ATEX vs IECEx

| | ATEX | IECEx |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Region | EU | Global |
| Standard | EN/ATEX directives | IEC 60079 |
| Acceptance | EU mandatory | Widely accepted |

## What we ship

The transmitter carries **Ex ia IIC T4** (intrinsic safety), suitable
for Zone 1 with an appropriate safety barrier. Premium models add **Ex d**
flameproof housings. Every unit ships with a conformity certificate.
