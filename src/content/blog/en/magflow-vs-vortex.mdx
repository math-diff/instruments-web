Electromagnetic (mag) and vortex flowmeters are the two most common
general-purpose flow instruments — but they are not interchangeable.

## Use electromagnetic when

- The liquid is **conductive** (water, slurries, acids with conductivity
  above ~5 µS/cm).
- You need **high accuracy on liquids** (±0.2% of rate).
- The pipe is large (up to DN600) and a wafer design keeps cost down.

## Use vortex when

- The media is **steam, gas or non-conductive liquid**.
- You need **high temperature** (up to 350 °C, e.g. steam lines).
- The flow has reasonable velocity — vortex needs a minimum Reynolds number.

## Quick comparison

| | Magflow | Vortex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Media | Conductive liquid only | Steam, gas, liquid |
| Accuracy | ±0.2% rate | ±1.0% rate |
| Max temp | 150 °C | 350 °C |
| Min conductivity | ~5 µS/cm | n/a |
| Cost (DN100) | Lower | Moderate |

## Rule of thumb

> Water and slurries → magflow. Steam and gas → vortex. When in doubt, send us
> your media data sheet.
