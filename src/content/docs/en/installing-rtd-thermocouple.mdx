RTDs and thermocouples convert temperature into an electrical signal;
wiring and installation quality directly determine signal quality.
This guide covers assembly and mineral-insulated (MI) sensors,
three-wire wiring, compensation cable and hazardous-area practice.

## Installation essentials

- **Insertion depth**: the measuring tip should sit near the pipe
  centerline; in liquids, effective immersion ≥ 15–20 sheath
  diameters, more in gases. Shallow insertion produces a systematic
  low reading.
- **Orientation**: insert at 45° against the flow or in an elbow
  downstream, ensuring good contact with the media.
- **Thermowell selection**:
  - Water, steam and mild media: 304/316L stainless;
  - High-velocity dusty flows (coal milling, powder conveying):
    **wear-resistant** type with reinforced tip;
  - Strong corrosion: Hastelloy or ceramic protection per media.
- **Response time**: mineral-insulated (MI) elements are slim and
  respond fast, suited to dynamic measurements; assembly versions
  with thermowells suit high pressure and erosion.

## RTD wiring: why three wires

A Pt100 element changes only ~0.39 Ω/°C — the same order as lead
resistance. Two 10 m copper leads of 1 mm² add ~0.36 Ω total, nearly
1 °C of error that drifts with ambient temperature.

**Three-wire** connection places one lead in each of two adjacent
bridge arms so lead resistance cancels; **four-wire** (constant
current + Kelvin measurement) eliminates it entirely and is reserved
for laboratory-grade accuracy. Field junction boxes are normally
wired three-wire.

Wiring checklist:

1. Connect the three leads to the matching terminals (A/B/C or
   1/2/3) — **swapping two leads on the same side cancels the
   compensation**;
2. Tighten all terminals; contact resistance must be minimal;
3. Ground the cable shield at **one end only** (usually at the
   instrument or cabinet side, never at the sensor end).

## Thermocouple wiring: compensation cable is critical

Thermocouples output millivolts and the reference (cold) junction
temperature participates in the calculation:

1. **Always use the matching compensation cable** (KX-type cable for
   Type K) with strict polarity — reversing it doubles the error;
2. Keep the thermocouple-to-cable junction within the cable rating
   (standard compensation cable insulation is rated 70 °C or
   105 °C); above that, use high-temperature extension cable;
3. **Never run compensation cable in the same conduit as power
   cables** — coupling noise corrupts the millivolt signal;
4. Enable cold-junction compensation in the display instrument or
   PLC.

## Hazardous-area installation

Flameproof (Ex d) RTDs and thermocouples in explosive atmospheres
must be installed per their certificates and the GB/T 3836 series:

- **Flameproof joints must never be painted, scratched or damaged**;
  replace aged sealing rings;
- Use cable glands rated for the protection level; plug unused
  entries with certified stoppers;
- After maintenance, restore the integrity of the flameproof paths;
  never open covers live.

## Troubleshooting quick table

| Symptom | RTD likely cause | Thermocouple likely cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jumping reading | Loose terminal / shield grounded both ends | Loose compensation joint / coupled noise |
| Consistent low offset | Shallow insertion / fouling | Compensation cable polarity reversed |
| Over-range display | Open circuit (incl. one lead of three) | Open thermocouple |
| Drift with load | Lead resistance varying (two-wire) | Cold-junction compensation disabled |
