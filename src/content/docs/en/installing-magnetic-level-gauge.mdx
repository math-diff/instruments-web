Magnetic bypass level gauges are rugged and nearly maintenance-free,
but poor installation — verticality errors, wrong valve sequence,
density mismatch — can still jam the float or distort the reading.
This guide covers the complete path from unboxing to service.

## Pre-installation checks

- **Density match**: the float waterline depends on media density.
  The density declared at order time must match the real service —
  changes beyond roughly ±0.02 g/cm³ introduce visible error;
- **Connection standard**: the chamber's upper and lower flanges mate
  with the vessel side nozzles, forming a communicating vessel; check
  flange standard and pressure rating against the vessel;
- **Length**: the chamber center-to-center dimension is ordered to
  the vessel level range and **must never be cut on site**.

## Installation steps

1. **Vertical mounting**: the chamber must be **truly vertical** —
   check with a spirit level in two perpendicular directions; tilt
   causes float binding against the wall and scale errors;
2. **Isolation valves**: fit an **isolation valve at both the top and
   bottom** connections (drain valve downstream of the bottom valve)
   for commissioning and maintenance;
3. **Flange sealing**: select gaskets per media and temperature
   (oil-resistant asbestos / PTFE / spiral-wound metal), tighten
   bolts evenly in a cross pattern;
4. **Scale orientation**: rotate the indicator scale to face the
   inspection walkway;
5. **Transmitter module (optional)**: magnetic switches or the
   4–20 mA module clip onto the chamber at the required setpoints;
   the switch reed orientation must match the float magnet polarity.

## Commissioning and drain sequence

Correct valve sequencing protects the float from impact:

**Putting into service (vessel from empty to pressurized):**

1. Slowly open the **top valve** first — vapor or liquid enters the
   chamber and equalizes pressure;
2. Then slowly open the **bottom valve** — liquid rises in the
   chamber and flags flip progressively;
3. Fully open both valves and check flange tightness.

**Draining / maintenance:**

1. Close the bottom valve, then the top valve;
2. Open the drain valve to depressurize and empty the chamber;
3. Disassemble only after confirming zero pressure in the chamber.

> Never disassemble any part of the chamber while pressurized.

## Media suitability

| Media condition | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Water, oils, solvents (density ≥ 0.65 g/cm³) | Standard type |
| Strong acids/bases and corrosives | Corrosion-protected type (PTFE/PFA lining or Hastelloy chamber) |
| Crystallizing / fouling media | Periodic draining and flushing; for heavy crystallization prefer radar |
| High temperature (e.g. saturated steam service) | High-temperature float with finned cooling section — state temperature at order |
| Unstable density (mixing vessels) | Prefer radar or another non-contact principle |

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause and fix |
| --- | --- |
| Flags stuck over a section | Float jammed — drain and flush; or flag magnets degraded — replace scale |
| Local reading offset by a fixed amount | Media density differs from order — re-zero or change float |
| Reading varies strongly with temperature | Density varies with temperature — inherent principle; order for the actual service temperature |
| Transmitted signal jumping | Module reed orientation wrong vs float magnet — flip and re-clip |
| No flags move at all | Valves closed / chamber empty; or float magnet installed reversed |

> When ordering, provide media name, density, operating temperature
> and pressure, level range and flange standard via the
