Electromagnetic flowmeters measure voltage induced in a conductive liquid.
Any stray voltage becomes measurement error — which makes grounding the most
common cause of magflow problems in the field.

## The rule

> The fluid must be at the same potential as the sensor body at the
> measurement point.

You achieve this with **grounding rings** (or grounding electrodes built into
the sensor).

## Correct installation

1. Install grounding rings on **both** flanges, between the flange and the
   liner.
2. Connect the rings and the sensor body to the same local earth point with
   low-impedance braided strap (≤ 0.1 Ω).
3. Do not rely on pipe thread or bolts for the earth path — corrosion breaks
   it within months.

## Common mistakes

- **Bonding the rings to a remote earth** instead of the local sensor body —
  introduces ground-loop voltage.
- **Skipping rings on plastic or lined pipe** — the fluid floats, readings
  drift.
- **Sharing the earth with variable-frequency drives** — VFD noise couples
  directly into the signal.

## Symptom checklist

| Symptom | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Reading drifts with pump on/off | Ground loop |
| Empty-pipe alarm stuck on | Liner wetted? Check grounding |
| Output fixed at 0 or max | Signal cable shield earthed both ends |
