Most of a bimetal thermometer's measurement error is introduced at
installation — insufficient insertion depth, a poorly chosen location
or a bad reading angle will make a perfectly good instrument read
unbelievable numbers. This guide walks the full installation process.

## Choosing the location

- Measure in a **flowing, thermally uniform** section: avoid dead
  zones, eddies downstream of valves and areas near heating elements.
- On piping, insert the stem **against the flow or perpendicular to
  it**, with the sensing tip at or near the pipe centerline.
- Avoid mounting on insulated cladding or strongly vibrating bodies;
  near pumps and rotating machinery, specify a **liquid-filled
  (damped) case**.

## Insertion depth: the first-order error factor

If the immersed depth is insufficient, heat conducts outward along
the stem and the reading reads systematically low (especially at high
temperatures). Engineering rules of thumb:

- **Liquids**: effective insertion depth ≥ 15–20 stem diameters;
- **Gases**: heat transfer is poorer, so go deeper or use an
  enlarged sensing tip.

Example: an 8 mm stem in liquid needs at least 120–160 mm of
immersion. On small pipes, install at a **45° angle against the
flow** or in an elbow to gain depth.

## Connection and mounting forms

| Form | Typical service |
| --- | --- |
| Threaded (M27×2 common) | Direct pipe insertion, most common |
| Union nut | Periodic withdrawal for calibration |
| Flange | Vessel nozzles, quick-open sealing |

Seal threads with PTFE tape or a gasket. When tightening, **hold the
hex of the connection boss with a wrench — never twist the case**,
or the movement inside will be damaged.

## Dial orientation and reading

- **Axial** (pointer parallel to stem): panel and cabinet mounting;
- **Radial** (pointer perpendicular to stem): vertical pipe runs,
  facing the inspection walkway.

Read with the eye perpendicular to the dial; readings between
graduations are taken at the nearest line.

## Verification and maintenance

- A bimetal thermometer is purely mechanical: **no power, no
  configuration** — it works as soon as it is installed.
- Periodic verification against a reference thermometer or a dry-block
  calibrator at 25%, 50% and 75% of span is common practice.
- Sustained drift beyond the accuracy class calls for **replacement
  of the whole instrument** (the movement has no field adjustment).
- Fouling on the thermowell outer wall lengthens response time;
  clean it periodically.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Low reading, stable | Insufficient insertion / fouled sheath |
| Pointer vibrates, unreadable | Ambient vibration — change to liquid-filled type |
| Leak at the connection | Case was twisted during install — re-seal holding the hex |
| Sudden dead pointer | Movement damaged or stem bent — replace instrument |
